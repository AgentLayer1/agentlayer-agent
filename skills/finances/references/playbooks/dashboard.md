# Dashboard

**You own that the finances page tells the truth about the statements behind it, and that every line ends up in the right category, approved by the operator.** "Refresh my finances", "open the finances dashboard", "categorise the new transactions", a pasted ```` ```finances-edits ```` block, a stated preference ("Booking.com flights are travel"), "why was October high?", or the end of an [import](import.md).

The categories live in `RULES.md` at the project root. Its prose (`## How I categorise`, `## Categories I use`) is the operator's, and adds to the skill's [categories](../categories.md), winning where they disagree. Its `## Learned` blocks are the exact patterns the engine applies on every build. Approval lives on a learned rule: approving one line approves every line that rule covers, past and future. `bun "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/rules.ts" init` writes the file from its template when the project has none.

1. **Render:** `bun "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/page.ts"` fetches any exchange rates it's missing (offline it keeps what it has and says what's missing) and rebuilds `dashboard.html` from `data/` and `RULES.md`. Never edit the page, its data block, or a learned block by hand; change the input through the scripts and render again.
2. **Read the page's health first.** The page's data block (`finance-data`) carries `checks` (shown on the page's Health tab), `insights` and `approval`; read them from the rendered file rather than recomputing. A health item that fails is said before any figure, with its fix (the statement to download, the month to re-import). Call them health items to the operator, never "checks", which reads as bank cheques.
3. **Apply a pasted block** before anything else, when the operator pastes one: save it to a scratch file and run `bun "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/rules.ts" apply <file>`. It confirms the approved rules, writes each fix (for the payee, or for that line alone), and reports anything it couldn't resolve; say those back rather than guessing. Render, and reply with the approved share before and after.
4. **Record a stated preference** in `RULES.md`'s `## How I categorise`, in the operator's words, one bullet per rule. Then categorise again (step 5) so the learned rules follow it. A preference that contradicts an approved rule is raised, not silently applied: approved decisions are the operator's.
5. **Categorise** (sync runs this for new payees too):
   - Run `bun "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/rules.ts" review`: the payees no rule covers, biggest first, with sample descriptions, accounts, entities, and the operator's approved rules as examples.
   - Read the skill's [categories](../categories.md), then `RULES.md`'s prose, which wins. Use only those categories (the operator's list when `## Categories I use` names one).
   - For each payee you can judge from evidence (the merchant, the description, a matched receipt, the approved examples), propose a rename when the bank's text is noisy (`{ match: "^GRAB RIDES", payee: "Grab rides" }`, a pattern that also catches future lines) and a category (`{ payee: "Grab rides", category: "transport" }`). Add `for:` only from evidence that the expense belongs to another entity (a school fee on a company card). Turn prose into patterns where the bank's text tells cases apart: "Grab rides are transport, GrabFood is food" becomes two rules. Where it can't, leave the line.
   - **Scope a rule to its entity** (`entity: "<slug>"`, on a rename or a category rule) whenever its pattern could also fit another entity's text: the operator's own name, a family member's, "Fund Transfer", a shared merchant. A company's "transfer to the director" rule unscoped also catches the director's own transfers between personal accounts. `account: "<id>"` narrows to one account the same way.
   - Never guess a payment to a person (a name, "Fund Transfer"), a cash withdrawal, or a payee you can't place: leave it for the operator. A transfer to the operator's own name at another bank is `transfer`.
   - Save them with `bun "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/rules.ts" suggest <file>`, the JSON `{ "payees": [...], "categories": [...] }`. They are added unconfirmed. A suggestion for a target that already has an unconfirmed rule replaces it (`replaced` in the report); one the operator approved is skipped, so a suggestion never overwrites a decision.
   - Retract a suggestion with `bun "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/rules.ts" drop <file>`, the same JSON naming each rule's `match` or `payee` (and its `entity` or `account` when scoped). It removes only unconfirmed rules; an approved one is listed under `kept` for the operator to change on the page. Never edit a learned block by hand.
   - Render. The page shows them as suggested, and the operator approves or fixes them on its Categorise page.
6. **Answer from the data.** A question about the money ("why was October high?", "what do we spend on food?") is answered from the data block's `lines` and `recurring`, with the period, the payees and the amounts that explain it, and a pointer to the page that shows it (Overview, Month, October). Say how much of the period is approved; a figure from suggestions is one the operator hasn't confirmed. A period without statements is said to be missing.

**Reply:** the health items that fail and their fix, what changed (lines imported, suggestions added, edits applied, and the approved share before and after), anything an edit couldn't resolve, and the page's path (`projects/finances/dashboard.html`) for the operator to open.
